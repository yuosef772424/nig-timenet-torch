## What the model learned

- **DW did what it was told on the training days.** It put 38 % of the total weight on the top 10 % of days (mean of
  14 fold x seed tables); the heaviest days of the last fold are the crashes: 2022-05-09 .. 12 (LUNA), 2022-11-08 / 09
  (FTX), 2024-04-12 / 13, 2024-12-09 (weight 5.8-6.2 x an equal day, at the cap).
- **But the "failure" it measured is the day's volatility.** The day weight's Spearman correlation with the day's mean
  |close label| (dispersion) is 0.93, with |market move| 0.53, with the market's sign -0.01 and with the rows of the day
  -0.04. Mean weight by dispersion quintile: 0.27 / 0.38 / 0.59 / 1.02 / 2.73 (calm days ten times lighter than wild
  ones); by market quintile 1.71 / 0.68 / 0.59 / 0.69 / 1.34 (both large up and large down days). The NIG NLL of a
  wide day is high whatever the model does, so the weighting is "train mostly on volatile days".
- **What it gained on TEST**: the long book on the wild third of days (+0.346 % a day vs R +0.225 %) and on down days
  (-1.06 % vs -1.14 %), and a better lowest decile of day IC on both seeds. R's close rank IC by day stays above DW in
  6 of 7 folds (all but fold 4).
- BAL (balance only) raises the weight of the days with fewer rows (inference: the early years, fewer coins; not
  measured per year here): no gain on any metric.

## Bias

Share of rows with mu > 0 against the realised share of up closes (48.6 %): R 48.2 %, DW 47.8 %, BAL 52.7 %. On down
days every arm predicts up for 52-57 % of rows while 29.6 % go up; on up days 45-51 % against 67.5 %: the close mean
does not follow the market day in any arm (known: the timing lives in the level across timestamps, not in the share).
BAL leans long (its weight moves to the early, mostly rising years). No correction fitted (no VAL rule was
pre-registered for it).

## Selective use

Only the pre-registered book was read (VAL 90th percentile of mu per fold, every TEST row above it; one rule, 3 arms x 2
seeds). DW's gain sits in the wild third of days (+0.12 % a day over R) and is lost on calm / mid days: that split was
found on TEST, so it is a hypothesis for the next walk-forward (H2 below), not a result.

## Why it failed

**Hidden by the measure of failure**: the day loss that drives the weights is the NIG NLL, whose level is set by the
day's volatility (Spearman 0.93 with the day's dispersion), not by how wrong the model is relative to what that day
allows. The weighting therefore became volatility weighting: calm days got a quarter of an equal share, the ranking on
calm and mid days (where most of R's IC lives) fell, and the NLL got worse in every group because the wide days that
dominate the loss pull the predicted scale and the mean toward their own errors (sigma 3 % narrower, mean NLL +0.02 to
+0.03 with a CI above 0). The long-book gain on wild days is real on both seeds but small against the seed spread
(paired CI around 0) and does not make the book positive without its best 5 % of days.

## Next hypotheses

1. **H1 (scale-free failure)**: measure a day's failure relative to what the day allows: the excess of the day's loss
   over a trivial predictor's loss on the same day (constant mu, the day's realised sd), or the day's rank-IC
   shortfall (1 - day IC) for the close head. Refuted if the day weight still correlates with dispersion above 0.5, or
   if the lowest-decile day IC and the mean IC do not both rise on 2 seeds. Cheapest test: a new failure signal behind
   the same switch (a registry entry next to the NLL one), E1 + a 2-seed walk-forward of R vs DW-H1.
2. **H2 (gentler weighting)**: strength 0.5 and max_ratio 2 (calm days keep at least half a share). Refuted if the IC
   on calm / mid days still falls more than 0.002 against R or the wild-day long gain disappears. Cheapest test: two
   arms on the same walk-forward (config only).
3. **H3 (regime use, from the TEST split above)**: a long book taken only on days whose VAL-fitted dispersion forecast
   is in the top third, with DW against R. Refuted if the gain over R on those days is not above 0 on both seeds of a
   new walk-forward.
